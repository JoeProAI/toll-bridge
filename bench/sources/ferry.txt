The island ferry will add a fourth daily round trip on weekdays beginning in May, the regional transit board decided on Tuesday.

Ridership on the route reached 412,000 passengers last year, up 19 percent from the year before and above the level the route carried before 2020.

The new sailing will leave the mainland terminal at 6:15 in the morning, a time that commuters who work early hospital shifts had asked for in a survey last fall.

Adding the trip will cost about $1.4 million a year in fuel, crew and maintenance, and fares are expected to cover roughly 45 percent of that.

The rest will come from the island's tourism tax, which the county raised by half a percentage point in January.

The board also approved a study of a hybrid electric vessel to replace the oldest boat in the fleet, which entered service in 1987.
