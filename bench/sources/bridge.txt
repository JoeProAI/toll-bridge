State inspectors rated the Mill Street bridge in poor condition after finding cracked steel and exposed rebar during their routine inspection in June.

The bridge carries about 14,000 vehicles a day across the river and is the only crossing within three miles that can handle fire trucks.

Engineers lowered the bridge's weight limit to 15 tons, which keeps loaded school buses and most delivery trucks off the span until repairs are done.

The city estimates a full replacement would cost $28 million and take about two years, while a deck repair would cost $6 million and last about fifteen years.

The council voted 5 to 2 to apply for a federal bridge grant that would cover up to 80 percent of a replacement.

If the grant is awarded, construction could begin in the spring of 2028, with a temporary bridge keeping one lane open in each direction.
